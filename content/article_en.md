# What is the cost of a poor digital experience?

Companies talk more and more about automation, AI and scale. But in practice, poor experience still appears in very concrete signals: complaints, low resolution, poor scores and response delays.

This project started from a simple question: if digital experience leaves traces, can we measure those signals and turn them into business intelligence?

To answer that, I analyzed 1.380.388 public complaints from Consumidor.gov.br, using the monthly files from January to April 2026 and finalization date as the main cut.

## Why public complaints matter

A public complaint does not represent the entire customer experience. It is a limited cut. Still, it is valuable because it shows where friction became strong enough for the consumer to formally register a problem.

The goal is not to accuse sectors. The goal is to identify friction signals that can guide prioritization, investigation and operational improvement.

## What was measured

The analysis combines four dimensions:

1. Relative complaint volume
2. Non-resolution rate among evaluated complaints
3. Low satisfaction based on the consumer score
4. Average response time

In the overall cut, the response rate was 84.3%, the resolution rate among evaluated complaints was 37.3%, the average score was 2.37 and the average response time was 6.3 days.

## The Digital Friction Index

To compare sectors, I created the DFI, Digital Friction Index. It ranges from 0 to 100 and combines the four signals with simple and documented weights:

- 30% relative volume
- 30% non-resolution
- 25% low satisfaction
- 15% response delay

The DFI is not an official score and should not be read as an absolute truth. It is an analytical proxy to compare friction signals and raise better hypotheses.

## Main findings

In the analyzed period, the sector with the highest DFI was Bancos, Financeiras e Administradoras de Cartão, with an index of 78.2 and 585,833 complaints.

The most frequent problem group was Cobrança / Contestação, with 736,791 records.

A relevant contrast also appeared: Empresas de Serviços Postais e Logística combined above-median resolution with below-median satisfaction. This is important because solving a case does not necessarily mean delivering a good experience.

## What companies can learn

A poor experience costs more than a complaint. It consumes time, creates rework, increases pressure on support teams, reduces trust and may reveal structural problems in the journey.

The real value of this analysis is not the ranking itself. It is the ability to turn scattered signals into better questions:

- Where is friction concentrated?
- Is the problem volume, resolution, satisfaction or delay?
- Is there a sector with good resolution but poor perceived quality?
- What should be investigated first?

## Limitations

More complaints do not automatically mean worse experience. A sector with a larger customer base tends to have more records. Also, not every consumer complains publicly and not every poor experience appears in the dataset.

That is why the DFI should be used as an exploratory reading, not as a final judgment.

## Conclusion

Poor digital experience does not appear only in satisfaction surveys. It appears in time, rework, recurrence, low resolution and consumer frustration.

The point of this project is to show how public data can be treated, organized and transformed into an intelligence product. Not just a dashboard. Method, narrative and decision.
